# tsdb 0.11.0 benchmark results (2026-11-26)

This record updates the [0.9.0 release benchmark](0.9.0.md) for **tsdb 0.11.0** (`1171dfff`, disk format 9) and adds
two new benchmark suites introduced in 0.11.0:

1. **Single-node comparison (100 million rows, server and embedded, plus 31 compression datasets):** re-measured for
   tsdb 0.11.0 on the exact same machine, input files, and query specifications as the 0.9.0 release benchmark. Every
   answer was checked against the 0.9.0 reference dumps (0 mismatches across all query classes, LTTB, and both step and
   linear time-weighted averages). The other eleven databases were not re-run; their numbers are the unchanged 0.9.0
   baselines from [0.9.0.md](0.9.0.md) and [0.9.0-tables.md](0.9.0-tables.md).
2. **Codec throughput and compression across three CPU microarchitectures (v0.11.0):** single-thread `decode-bench`,
   `codec-bench`, and `type-bench` on AMD Ryzen 9 7950X (Zen 4, AVX-512), Intel Xeon Gold 6148 (Skylake, AVX2 kernels;
   tsdb intentionally selects AVX2 on Skylake to avoid AVX-512 frequency downclocking), and Intel Xeon E5-2697 v4
   (Broadwell, AVX2), comparing pre-0.11.0 code against v0.11.0 (AVX2/AVX-512 SIMD kernels plus format-9 `int-for`,
   `bool-pack`, `ts-for-bp`, and dividing scales).
3. **Multi-node cluster ingestion (v0.11.0):** sustained durable (`fsync` on) cluster writes across both a 4-node
   homogeneous all-NVMe cluster and a 5-node mixed-storage cluster, comparing 40 GbE TCP against 56 Gbps InfiniBand RDMA
   at `replication = 3` and `replication = 1`.

## How single-node tsdb 0.11.0 was run

- **Machine:** AMD Ryzen 9 7950X (16 cores, 32 threads), 64 GB RAM, NVMe disk (ext4 on LUKS), CPU governor
  `performance`, quiet system (load average 0.05 at start).
- **Build:** `make && make -C bench/dbcmp` at `1171dfff` (`v0.11.0`, disk format 9, `simd avx512`, `io_uring`,
  `--no-auth`, `fsync` on for every write).
- **Repetitions:** 5 runs per measurement. Loads were run 5 times with a sync and settle pause between runs so NVMe
  writeback had cleared (matching the rested load protocol of 0.9.0). Whole-table queries, TWA, and LTTB were repeated
  5 times after a warm-up. Random windows and 100-series batches ran 5 × 5 s after a 2 s warm-up.
- **Verification:** `check.py` and `rel.py` verified 0 mismatches across `raw`, `agg1d`, `agg7d`, `point` (200 windows
  each), `full0`, `full3600`, `full60` (all 100 series), `lttb` (100,000 points), `twa` (339,774 buckets), and `twal`
  (339,774 buckets, max relative difference 3.8e-16).

## 100 million rows: server (`tsdbd`, TCP, `io_uring`, `fsync` on)

### Load

5 rested loads through 4 connections (`batches of 1,000,000`, `fsync` on before acknowledgement):

- **Mean load time:** **1.11 s** (`1.1126 s`, range 1.00–1.47 s; median **1.04 s** (`1.0368 s`) = **96.5 M rows/s**;
  best run 1.00 s = **99.8 M rows/s**).
- **Throughput (100 M / mean load time):** **89.9 M rows/s** (up from 85.8 M rows/s in 0.9.0; 1.9× faster than
  ClickHouse's 47.5 M rows/s without fsync).
- **Settle time after load:** 0.00 s (merges completed during ingest; 129.0 MB settled, 1.290 B/row; 115.8 MB after
  `tsdb_optimize`).

## Codec throughput and compression across three CPUs (v0.11.0)

Version 0.11.0 adds AVX2/AVX-512 SIMD kernels (and fixes AVX2 bit-unpacking dispatch on AVX-512 hosts, where pre-0.11.0
code fell back to scalar loops), fast prefix-sum & decimal reconstruction, and format-9 encodings (`BLK_INT_FOR`
frame-of-reference + bitpacking for `int64`, `BLK_BOOL_PACK` 1-bit/RLE packing for `bool` and small `state`,
`BLK_TS_FOR_BP` for jittery timestamps, and dividing scales). On Intel Skylake, tsdb intentionally selects AVX2 kernels
to avoid AVX-512 frequency downclocking.

Single-thread throughput (pinned to CPU 0, mean of 5 rounds, quiet machines) comparing pre-0.11.0 (`93b42f03`) against
v0.11.0 (`7fa5c656`):

## Multi-node cluster ingestion (v0.11.0)

Real multi-node clusters running `tsdbd --cluster --io uring --workers 32` with durable writes (`fsync` on every batch
before acknowledgement), balanced leaders (2 volumes per node), client drivers running on separate hosts over 40 GbE
Ethernet (`batch = 250,000`, `pipe = 4`, 4 connections per volume, 2 s warm-up, 12 s sustained, **0 errors** across
all runs). Peer replication links used either 40 GbE TCP (`--peer-rdma off`) or native 56 Gbps InfiniBand RDMA verbs
(`--peer-rdma on`, verified `tcp 0 rdma N` on every peer link class after the run).

### 4-node homogeneous all-NVMe cluster (4× dual-socket Intel Xeon Gold 6148, NVMe)

8 replicated volumes (2 leaders per node, 32 client connections total):

| configuration | wire compression | aggregate net throughput | physical replica writes | rows committed (12 s) | client wire bandwidth | median batch p50 | max node p99 |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `replication = 3`, 40 GbE TCP | ZSTD | **185.5 M rows/s** | ~556 M/s | 2.25 billion | 260 MB/s | 180.9 ms | 319.2 ms |
| `replication = 3`, 56 Gbps IB RDMA | ZSTD | **189.9 M rows/s** | ~570 M/s | 2.30 billion | 266 MB/s | 181.9 ms | **228.2 ms** (−29%) |
| `replication = 3`, 40 GbE TCP | raw | **153.8 M rows/s** | ~461 M/s | 1.87 billion | 1,760 MB/s | 245.5 ms | 469.0 ms |
| `replication = 3`, 56 Gbps IB RDMA | raw | **156.1 M rows/s** | ~468 M/s | 1.90 billion | 1,787 MB/s | 247.3 ms | **286.0 ms** (−39%) |

At `replication = 3` over InfiniBand RDMA with ZSTD wire compression, individual NVMe nodes sustained **40.9–64.4 M net
replicated rows/s** each (**123–193 M physical replica writes/s per node**), and RDMA reduced worst-node p99 latency by
29% (compressed) and 39% (raw) compared to 40 GbE TCP.

### 5-node mixed-storage cluster (4× Xeon Gold 6148 NVMe + 1× Xeon E5-2697 v4 SATA SSD)

10 volumes (2 leaders per node, 40 client connections total):

| configuration | wire compression | aggregate net throughput | rows committed (12 s) | client wire bandwidth | notes |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| `replication = 1`, 40 GbE TCP | ZSTD | **388.5 M rows/s** | 4.69 billion | 545 MB/s | 4 NVMe nodes: **352.7 M/s** (78.0–97.2 M/s each); SATA SSD node: 35.7 M/s |
| `replication = 1`, 40 GbE TCP | raw | **352.6 M rows/s** | 4.26 billion | **4,035 MB/s** (32.3 Gbps) | 4 NVMe nodes: 69.8–91.0 M/s each; SATA SSD node: 25.8 M/s |
| `replication = 3`, 40 GbE TCP | ZSTD | **132.6 M rows/s** | 1.62 billion | 186 MB/s | 2 pure-NVMe leaders: **46.6–48.4 M/s**; 3 leaders with a SATA replica: 10.9–15.8 M/s |
| `replication = 3`, 56 Gbps IB RDMA | ZSTD | **132.9 M rows/s** | 1.62 billion | 187 MB/s | 2 pure-NVMe leaders: **46.7–48.6 M/s** (p99 183–188 ms vs 213 ms TCP) |
| `replication = 3`, 40 GbE TCP | raw | **104.0 M rows/s** | 1.28 billion | 1,191 MB/s | 2 pure-NVMe leaders: 37.9–40.2 M/s; SATA-constrained leaders: 7.5–10.9 M/s |
| `replication = 3`, 56 Gbps IB RDMA | raw | **105.5 M rows/s** | 1.30 billion | 1,208 MB/s | 2 pure-NVMe leaders: 38.0–41.3 M/s (p99 229–252 ms vs 246–361 ms TCP) |

In the mixed-storage `replication = 3` runs, synchronous durability (`fsync` on the full ISR before ack) meant the 3
leaders whose replica sets included the SATA SSD node ran at SATA `fsync` speed, while the 2 leaders placed solely on
NVMe nodes ran at full NVMe speed — illustrating why production clusters should match storage tiers across quorum
members.
